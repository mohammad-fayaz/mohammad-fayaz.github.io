## Abstract (English)

**Background and Objective:**  
Spiritual health is considered one of the fundamental dimensions of human health and may influence psychological and behavioral outcomes. Sexual self-efficacy refers to an individual’s perceived ability to function effectively within intimate relationships. This study aimed to investigate the correlation between spiritual health and sexual self-efficacy among pregnant women.

**Methods:**  
This descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted among pregnant women attending health centers in Sanandaj, Iran. A total of 384 participants were selected using cluster random sampling. Data were collected using a demographic checklist, Narenji’s Spiritual Health Questionnaire, Waziri and Lotfi Kashani’s Sexual Self-Efficacy Scale, and the ENRICH marital satisfaction questionnaire. Statistical analyses included descriptive statistics, t-tests, Pearson correlation, and regression modeling.

**Results:**  
The mean spiritual health score was 310.0 ± 4.33, and the mean sexual self-efficacy score was 458.0 ± 4.26. A significant positive correlation was observed between spiritual health and sexual self-efficacy (r = 0.72, P < 0.05). Regression analysis indicated that spiritual health explained approximately 51% of the variance in sexual self-efficacy.

**Conclusion:**  
The findings suggest that higher spiritual health is associated with greater sexual self-efficacy among pregnant women, highlighting its potential role in promoting psychological and relational well-being.
