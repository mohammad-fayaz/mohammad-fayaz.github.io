## Abstract

This study aimed to construct blood pressure (BP) percentiles according to age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) using data from a nationally representative sample of Iranian adults.The analyses were based on data collected from 8,425 adults aged 25–69 years. Demographic characteristics, anthropometric measurements, and blood pressure data were recorded for each participant. Linear regression models were used to assess the relationships between BMI, height, weight, age, and blood pressure. Separate percentile models were developed for systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) in men and women. Results showed that both SBP and DBP increased with age and BMI. Blood pressure was positively associated with weight and negatively associated with height. The findings indicate that overweight and obese individuals are more likely to fall within hypertensive blood pressure ranges. These age- and BMI-specific blood pressure percentiles may provide useful reference values for population health monitoring and clinical assessment.
