## Title

Blood Pressure Percentiles by Age and Height for Children and Adolescents in Tehran, Iran

## Authors

N. Ataei, M. Hosseini, **Mohammad Fayaz**, I. Navidi, A. Taghiloo, K. Kalantari, and F. Ataei

## Journal Information

**Journal:** Journal of Human Hypertension  
**Year:** 2015  
**Study Period:** 2000–2010 (Tehran, Iran)

**DOI:** 10.1038/jhh.2015.52

## Abstract

This study aimed to construct reference blood pressure (BP) percentiles by sex, age, and height for children and adolescents in Iran using a large population-based sample from Tehran. Data from 16,972 healthy individuals aged 1 month to 18 years collected between 2000 and 2010 were analyzed.

Blood pressure increased steadily with both age and height in a similar pattern for boys and girls up to approximately 14 years of age. After puberty, systolic blood pressure (SBP) increased more prominently in boys, while diastolic blood pressure (DBP) increased slightly more in girls. Iranian BP percentiles were compared with international reference standards, including the U.S. Fourth Report and the German KiGGS study, revealing notable differences across age groups and percentiles.

For younger boys (≤6 years), the 95th percentile of SBP differed from U.S. reference values by 2–21 mmHg and from German values by up to 9 mmHg. For ages 7–15, Iranian values were slightly higher than both references. At ages 16–17, SBP values were lower than the U.S. reference but slightly higher than German standards. For DBP, Iranian values were generally lower than both comparison standards by 1–11 mmHg.

The findings suggest that internationally derived BP reference charts may not be fully applicable to Iranian children, and locally derived percentiles should be used for more accurate clinical assessment.
