## Title

TyG Index and Insulin Resistance in Beta-Thalassemia

## Authors

## Abstract

Insulin resistance (IR) contributes to abnormalities in glucose metabolism among patients with beta-thalassemia major (βTM). The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index has recently been proposed as a simple, inexpensive, and accessible surrogate marker for assessing insulin resistance. This study evaluated the utility of the TyG index for monitoring insulin resistance in βTM patients receiving chronic regular transfusion therapy.

A total of 90 βTM patients participated in the study, of whom 53.3% were male. The TyG index was calculated using fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and triglyceride (TG) measurements. Two assessments (TyG.1 and TyG.2) were performed with a two-year interval between evaluations. Agreement between the TyG index and the Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) was examined using limits of agreement (LOA) analysis.

At baseline, 14.4% of participants had impaired fasting glucose levels (100–125 mg/dL), increasing to 37.8% after two years. Based on the initial TyG assessment, 34.4% of patients were classified as insulin resistant, rising to 82.2% at follow-up. Significant differences were observed between TyG.1 and TyG.2 values and their respective cutoff points (P < 0.001). The TyG index demonstrated good agreement with HOMA-IR, supporting its potential role as a practical tool for detecting and monitoring insulin resistance in patients with βTM.
