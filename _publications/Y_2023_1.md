## Abstract

**Introduction:**  
The COVID-19 pandemic produced both adverse socio-economic impacts and measurable environmental changes, including reductions in air pollution. Prior studies have reported substantial decreases in nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) concentrations in multiple regions worldwide.

**Methods:**  
This study examines air pollution dynamics across Iran and its neighboring countries, covering approximately 7.4% of the global population. Satellite-based atmospheric composition data from the Copernicus Sentinel-5P (S5P) mission, accessed via Google Earth Engine, were analyzed for the period Q1 2019 to Q4 2021. Pollutants included CH₄, CO, H₂O, HCHO, NO₂, O₃, SO₂, and UV Aerosol Index (UVAI/AAI). Outlier detection was performed using depth-based functional methods. Statistical inference was conducted using two-sample t-tests, Wilcoxon tests, and interval-wise functional testing with control of the family-wise error rate.

**Results:**  
Significant reductions in NO₂ were observed in Q2 2020 compared with Q2 2019 in most countries, except Iraq, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar, and Kuwait. CO and HCHO did not show statistically significant changes. CH₄, O₃, and UVAI/AAI showed region-specific significant variations. In Q2 2020 vs Q2 2021 comparisons, significant NO₂ changes were observed in Iran, Armenia, Turkey, UAE, and Saudi Arabia, while CH₄ showed widespread significance except in Azerbaijan.

**Conclusion:**  
COVID-19 lockdowns induced heterogeneous environmental effects across countries, with significant reductions observed primarily in nitrogen dioxide concentrations.
