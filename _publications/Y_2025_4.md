## Abstract

### Introduction
Insulin resistance (IR) plays a central role in the pathophysiology of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), yet its long-term trajectory across different phenotypes and obesity statuses remains unclear. This study aimed to model longitudinal IR patterns in women with PCOS, those meeting an isolated PCOS criterion, and healthy controls.

### Methods
This population-based prospective study included 1,759 reproductive-aged women (18–49 years) from the Tehran Lipid and Glucose Study. Participants were categorized into PCOS, isolated PCOS criterion, and healthy control groups, and further stratified by BMI and waist circumference. Functional principal component analysis (FPCA) was used to model HOMA-IR and fasting insulin trajectories as sparse functional data.

### Results
Women with PCOS showed age and BMI differences compared with controls, while baseline HOMA-IR levels were similar. Trajectories of insulin resistance differed significantly by obesity status. Women with PCOS and obesity demonstrated a pronounced upward trend in insulin resistance over time, whereas non-obese PCOS participants showed stable or improving patterns. Healthy and isolated criterion groups exhibited relatively stable or mildly increasing trajectories, with steeper increases in the presence of obesity.

### Conclusion
This study highlights heterogeneous longitudinal trajectories of insulin resistance across PCOS phenotypes. Obesity plays a key role in worsening metabolic trajectories, while non-obese PCOS individuals may experience stabilization or improvement over time.
