## Abstract

*Background*: Standard blood pressure (BP) references in children and adolescents are increasingly recommended to be based on anthropometric measures. This study aimed to develop the first BP reference percentiles by age and weight for Iranian children and adolescents aged 3–18 years.
*Methods*: A total of 16,246 children and adolescents aged 3–18 years were included from three cross-sectional studies conducted in Tehran, Iran. Demographic data, anthropometric indices, and BP measurements were collected. Quantile regression models were used to evaluate the association between weight percentiles and systolic and diastolic blood pressure across age, sex, and corresponding weight percentiles. Based on these analyses, age- and sex-specific BP nomograms adjusted for weight were developed.
*Results*: All regression coefficients for weight percentiles were statistically significant in quantile regression models (P < 0.05), confirming a strong association between weight and blood pressure. BP percentiles increased steadily across weight categories in both sexes, with only minor differences between boys and girls. The weight-adjusted BP curves indicated that lean children had a higher estimated prevalence of hypertension, while overweight and obese children showed lower relative prevalence when using adjusted nomograms.
*Conclusion*: This study provides the first Iranian BP reference nomograms based on age and weight for children and adolescents. Weight-adjusted BP percentiles offer a more accurate framework for assessing hypertension risk and classification in pediatric populations.
