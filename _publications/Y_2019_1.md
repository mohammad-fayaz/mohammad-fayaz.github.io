## Abstract

*Purpose*: Hypercholesterolemia is a major modifiable risk factor for non-communicable diseases, and monitoring its trends over time is essential for public health planning. Most previous studies have been cross-sectional and did not assess temporal changes using age–period–cohort (APC) analysis. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of age, period, and birth cohort on the prevalence of borderline and high cholesterol levels in the Iranian adult population.
*Methods*: Data from 110,302 individuals aged 25–69 years were collected from five national cross-sectional surveys conducted between 1990 and 2011 (1990–91, 1999, 2003, 2007, and 2011). The intrinsic estimator model was used to assess age, period, and cohort effects on cholesterol prevalence.
*Results*: The prevalence of borderline cholesterol levels was lower in men than women (39.8% vs. 46.3%). Similarly, high cholesterol prevalence was 13.1% in men and 18.0% in women. Cholesterol levels generally increased with age, with peak prevalence occurring in mid to late adulthood before stabilizing or declining slightly in older ages. In women, increases were more pronounced after menopause. Cohort analysis showed a declining trend in both borderline and high cholesterol prevalence in more recent birth cohorts.
*Conclusion*: The findings demonstrate that age, period, and cohort effects significantly influence cholesterol levels in Iranian adults. These temporal factors should be considered in designing prevention and management strategies for hypercholesterolemia.
