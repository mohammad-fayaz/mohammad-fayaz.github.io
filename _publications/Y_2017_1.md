Blood Pressure Nomograms for Children and Adolescents by Age and Body Mass Index in Tehran, Iran

## Abstract

*Background*: Standard blood pressure (BP) references in children and adolescents should be constructed using anthropometric indices. This study aimed to develop BP reference percentiles based on body mass index (BMI) for Iranian children and adolescents.
*Methods*: Data from 16,246 children and adolescents aged 3–18 years, collected from three cross-sectional studies in Tehran, were analyzed. Demographic characteristics, anthropometric measurements, and BP values were included. Quantile regression models were used to assess the association between BMI percentiles and systolic and diastolic blood pressure across different age and sex groups. Based on these analyses, age- and sex-specific BP nomograms adjusted for BMI were constructed.
*Results*: BMI percentiles showed significant associations with both systolic and diastolic blood pressure in all quantile regression models (P < 0.05), confirming the importance of BMI adjustment in BP reference construction. BP percentiles increased steadily across BMI categories for both sexes, with only minor differences between boys and girls. Lean individuals were estimated to have a lower prevalence of hypertension, while overweight subjects showed higher prevalence when using BMI-adjusted BP curves.
*Conclusion*: This study provides the first Iranian BP reference nomograms based on age and BMI for children and adolescents. The findings suggest that BMI-adjusted BP curves offer a more accurate assessment of hypertension prevalence and a more reliable classification standard.
