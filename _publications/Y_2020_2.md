## Abstract

Road traffic injuries are strongly influenced by behavioral risk factors such as driving offenses and average vehicle speed. Effective policy design requires a comprehensive understanding of temporal and spatial traffic patterns. This study applied functional data analysis to investigate time-varying traffic behavior near two airports.

Hourly traffic count data were collected from four locations at the entrances of international and domestic airports between 2018 and 2019. Statistical analyses, including descriptive statistics, ANOVA, and functional data analysis, were used to assess patterns in driving offenses and traffic flow.

Results showed distinct hourly patterns in driving offenses across different road segments. Traffic behavior differed significantly between to-and-from airport directions (P < 0.05). Hour of day, weekday, airport type, direction, and their interactions were all statistically significant predictors of driving offenses. Average speed varied significantly by vehicle type and time of day (P < 0.05).

Conclusion: Traffic count data provide valuable insights for transportation safety policy. Functional data analysis revealed complex temporal patterns in driving behavior near airports. The findings suggest that implementing time-varying speed zones in high-traffic airport areas may improve road safety.
