## Abstract

Hypertension is a major public health challenge worldwide. Although numerous studies have evaluated its prevalence and associated risk factors, most have relied on cross-sectional designs and have not simultaneously examined temporal changes across age, period, and birth cohort dimensions. This study aimed to assess the 20-year dynamics of hypertension among Iranian adults using an age–period–cohort (APC) analytical framework.

Data from 74,155 individuals aged 25–60 years were obtained from five national health surveys conducted in 1990–1991, 1999, 2003, 2007, and 2011. The effects of age, period, and birth cohort on hypertension prevalence were analyzed using the intrinsic estimator model.

The prevalence of hypertension generally increased with age in both men and women. Period effects indicated that hypertension prevalence remained relatively stable between 1990 and 1999 but increased during the mid-1990s. Cohort analysis demonstrated an overall declining trend in hypertension prevalence among more recent birth cohorts, with a few exceptions in specific male and female cohorts. The age effect showed a nearly monotonic increase in hypertension prevalence, while cohort effects suggested progressive improvements across generations.

These findings demonstrate that age, period, and cohort factors independently influence hypertension prevalence and provide important insights into long-term epidemiological trends in Iran.
