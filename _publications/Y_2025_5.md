## Abstract

### Background
Comprehensive and timely estimation of cause-specific mortality is essential for understanding global health trends and informing policy. The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 study provides updated estimates of mortality across 292 causes in 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023.

### Methods
The study used the Cause of Death Ensemble model (CODEm) to estimate cause-specific mortality rates. Years of life lost (YLLs) were computed using standard life expectancy. Additional metrics included probability of death (70q0), mean age at death, and uncertainty intervals derived from 250 posterior draws. Data sources included vital registration systems, surveys, censuses, and surveillance datasets.

### Findings
COVID-19 temporarily became the leading global cause of age-standardised mortality in 2021 but declined by 2023, with ischaemic heart disease and stroke resuming dominance. Substantial declines were observed in communicable diseases such as tuberculosis, diarrhoeal diseases, and measles. However, non-communicable diseases and injuries remain major contributors to global mortality. Mean global age at death increased from 46.8 years in 1990 to 63.4 years in 2023, with substantial regional disparities.

### Interpretation
Despite significant progress in reducing mortality from communicable diseases, global health disparities persist. The findings highlight increasing non-communicable disease burdens in low- and middle-income regions and emphasize the need for strengthened health systems and targeted interventions.
