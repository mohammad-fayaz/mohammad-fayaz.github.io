## Abstract

### Background

EEGLAB is one of the most widely used open-source software environments for processing and analyzing electroencephalography (EEG) data. Due to its extensive plugin ecosystem, active international community, educational resources, and integration with MATLAB, EEGLAB has become one of the most influential computational neuroscience toolboxes.

### Methods

A bibliometric analysis was conducted using publications indexed in the **Web of Science (WOS)** database. A total of **12,700 publications** published between 2004 and 2023 were extracted and analyzed using the **Bibliometrix** package in R. The study examined publication trends, citation structures, collaboration networks, author productivity, keyword evolution, and influential journals, institutions, countries, and software extensions.

### Results

The dataset included:

- 12,700 publications
- 1,125 publication sources
- 29,125 authors
- 19,062 author keywords
- 13,707 Keywords Plus terms
- 279,617 cited references

Key indicators included:

- Annual growth rate: **28.12%**
- International co-authorship rate: **37.27%**
- Average co-authors per article: **4.89**
- Average citations per document: **22.51**

The most productive journals were:

- NeuroImage
- Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
- Scientific Reports
- Psychophysiology
- PLOS ONE

The United States, Germany, and the United Kingdom were the most cited countries. Among EEGLAB extensions, **ERPLAB**, **ADJUST**, and **ICLabel** demonstrated particularly strong citation performance.

### Conclusion

EEGLAB represents one of the most influential MATLAB toolboxes in computational neuroscience. Its scientific impact continues to expand globally through widespread adoption in both developed and developing countries. The bibliometric patterns identified in this study provide a comprehensive overview of the evolution of EEG research supported by EEGLAB and its ecosystem.

## Research Impact

This study provides one of the largest bibliometric investigations ever conducted on a scientific software platform in neuroscience. By examining more than twelve thousand publications, it maps the global scientific ecosystem built around EEGLAB and demonstrates its role in advancing electrophysiological research, neuroinformatics, brain-computer interfaces, cognitive neuroscience, clinical neuroscience, and machine learning applications involving EEG data.

The accompanying reproducibility files allow researchers to reproduce and extend the analyses using open bibliometric tools and workflows.
