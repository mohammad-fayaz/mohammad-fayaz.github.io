## Abstract

### Background

Percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) is the preferred surgical technique for treating kidney stones. Preoperative anxiety is a common psychiatric symptom experienced by many patients undergoing surgery. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of pregabalin administration on preoperative anxiety and postoperative pain in patients undergoing PCNL.

### Methods

This randomized controlled trial included 104 patients scheduled for PCNL surgery, randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo, 75 mg pregabalin, 150 mg pregabalin, or 300 mg pregabalin. Anxiety levels were measured using the Hamilton Rating Scale for Anxiety (HRSA), and postoperative pain was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS). Statistical analyses included the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Kruskal–Wallis test, generalized estimating equations (GEE), and Poisson log-linear models.

### Results

No statistically significant differences were observed between pregabalin doses and placebo in reducing preoperative anxiety (P = 0.931 and P = 0.886). However, postoperative pain decreased over time in all groups, with statistically significant reductions in most comparisons (P < 0.05).

### Conclusion

Pregabalin administration before PCNL surgery does not significantly reduce preoperative anxiety. However, it may be beneficial in reducing postoperative pain, with 75 mg identified as the most effective dose.
