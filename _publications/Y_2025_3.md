## Abstract

### Background

Drowning is the third leading cause of unintentional injury-related deaths worldwide. This study systematically analyzed drowning mortality and associated risk factors across 204 countries and territories using Global Burden of Disease 2021 data.

### Methods

Mortality data, years of life lost (YLLs), and risk factor attribution (ICD-10 codes W65–W74) from 1980 to 2021 were analyzed. Countries were grouped by Socio-demographic Index (SDI). The age–period–cohort (APC) model was used to estimate temporal effects and net drift, reflecting annual percentage change. Risk factor attribution included high alcohol use, high temperature, and occupational injuries.

### Results

In 2021, there were an estimated 233,906 premature deaths and 15.0 million YLLs due to drowning, representing substantial reductions since 1980. The global age-standardised mortality rate (ASMR) declined from 11.09 to 3.16 per 100,000. Low and low-middle SDI countries exhibited the highest mortality burden. Some countries, including China and India, showed significant reductions in mortality trends. While drowning mortality attributable to high temperature and occupational injury declined, mortality linked to high alcohol use increased in several low- and middle-SDI regions.

### Conclusion

Despite substantial global reductions, drowning remains a major preventable cause of premature mortality, particularly in lower SDI countries. Targeted interventions addressing modifiable risk factors are essential for further reduction.
