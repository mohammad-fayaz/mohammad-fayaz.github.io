## Abstract

### Introduction

Bagging (BG) and Random Forest (RF) are among the most widely used supervised statistical learning methods based on classification and regression trees. These methods can accommodate various response types, including continuous and categorical outcomes. However, many modern datasets contain complex covariates such as functional data, curves, time series, and hybrid structures combining functional and non-functional variables. Existing extensions of RF for functional data often rely on random-splitting techniques to summarize functional observations through informative statistics.

### Methods

This study extends the random-splitting framework and introduces the **Mixed-Data Bagging (MD-BG)** and **Mixed-Data Random Forest (MD-RF)** algorithms for datasets containing multiple functional and non-functional covariates simultaneously. The proposed methods support mixed and hybrid data structures and provide variable importance plots (VIPs) for assessing the contribution of each covariate.

### Results

The primary distinction between MD-BG and MD-RF lies in covariate selection. MD-BG retains all available predictors during model construction, whereas MD-RF randomly samples subsets of covariates at each split. The proposed MD-RF framework successfully identifies the most influential regions of functional predictors while simultaneously evaluating the importance of traditional scalar covariates.

### Conclusion

The proposed methods were evaluated using the **DTI** and **Tecator** datasets for both regression and classification tasks. Results demonstrate the utility of mixed-data random forests in handling complex functional and hybrid datasets. To facilitate practical implementation, the methods were incorporated into an open-source R package named **RSRF**.

## Research Impact

This work extends traditional Random Forest methodology to settings involving multiple functional and mixed-data covariates. The proposed framework enables machine learning analysis of complex biomedical, neuroimaging, environmental, and longitudinal datasets where conventional tree-based methods are difficult to apply directly.

The methodological developments introduced in this article formed the foundation for subsequent research on functional random forests, survival forests with functional predictors, and machine learning methods for neuroimaging applications.

**Authors:** Mohammad Fayaz, Alireza Abadi

##### Abstract

Functional Data Analysis (FDA) provides a framework in which observations are represented as functions rather than discrete measurements. In many practical applications, datasets contain both functional and non-functional covariates, requiring statistical learning approaches capable of handling mixed-data structures.

This work proposed a Functional Random Forest framework for datasets containing both functional and scalar predictors with continuous or categorical outcomes. Functional observations were transformed through random splitting mechanisms that preserved temporal and structural information. Additional extensions based on basis function representations, Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA), and Hybrid FPCA were investigated.

Simulation studies and benchmark datasets, including DTI and EEG-EMG studies, demonstrated improved predictive performance compared with conventional random forest approaches. Variable importance measures were also developed to identify influential functional regions and predictors.

This work represents one of the earliest developments that ultimately evolved into the Mixed-Data Random Forest methodology and the RSRF package.

**Presenter:** Mohammad Fayaz

**Co-authors:** Nezhat Shakeri, Alireza Abadi, Soheila Khodakarim

##### Abstract

Survival analysis methods are widely used for modeling time-to-event outcomes with censoring. In this study, random-splitting Bagging and Random Forest algorithms were extended to accommodate multiple functional covariates in survival analysis settings.

Functional covariates were randomly partitioned using intervals generated from exponential distributions, and summary statistics such as means and medians were incorporated into the tree-building process. A novel variable importance measure was also developed to identify the most influential regions of functional predictors.

The proposed methodology was applied to a publicly available EEG-fNIRS dataset involving a discrimination/selection response task. Event-related potentials derived from EEG recordings served as functional predictors, while fNIRS latency measurements were considered time-to-event outcomes.

The study demonstrated the potential of random-splitting survival forests for analyzing complex neuroimaging datasets containing high-dimensional functional covariates and further expanded the methodological framework underlying the RSRF package.
