## Abstract

**Purpose**: This study compares visual and auditory oddball tasks in healthy subjects using EEG data. The analysis considers three dimensions of EEG signals: regional (brain regions), longitudinal (stimulus repetition), and functional (entire ERP curves rather than only amplitude and latency). Trial-level effects were also investigated for each stimulus condition.
**Materials and Methods**: Seventeen healthy participants (11 males) were included. EEG data were obtained from an existing dataset and preprocessed. Each task included three trials with 25 target stimuli per group. Functional data analysis and hybrid principal component analysis (HPCA) were applied to estimate regional, longitudinal, and functional effects. Bayesian credible sets were computed for each trial. Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMM) were used to analyze trial effects.
**Results**: The first eigenfunctions for functional and longitudinal dimensions, as well as the first and second eigenvectors for regional dimensions, were estimated. Bayesian credible sets indicated variability across trials for different stimuli. GAMM results showed significant interaction effects in the functional dimension for both tasks. The longitudinal interaction effect was significant in the visual task but not in the auditory task. Brain region effects were significant, with strong effects observed in auditory tasks (P < 0.001), while they were not significant in visual tasks (P > 0.06). Region-by-stimulus interactions were significant in the occipital, parietal, and right temporal regions for the auditory task.
**Conclusion**: Functional data analysis methods, including HPCA and GAMM, effectively captured complex EEG dynamics across tasks. Brain regions exhibited different activity patterns between auditory and visual oddball tasks. Trial repetition influenced task performance, indicating the importance of modeling trial-level variability in EEG analysis.
