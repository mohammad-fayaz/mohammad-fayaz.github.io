## Abstract

### Aims

The prevalence of heart failure (HF) continues to rise, with increasing impact on younger populations. However, comprehensive epidemiological data on HF among adolescents and young adults remain limited. This study aimed to evaluate temporal trends and projections of HF burden among individuals aged 15–49 years from 1990 to 2021, with forecasts through 2035.

### Methods and Results

Data were obtained from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD 2021). We assessed temporal trends in HF prevalence and years lived with disability (YLDs) using estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) and age–period–cohort (APC) models. Associations with Sociodemographic Index (SDI) were evaluated using Pearson correlation. Future projections were generated using Bayesian age–period–cohort (BAPC) modeling.

Between 1990 and 2021, global HF prevalence among individuals aged 15–49 years increased by 76.76% (95% uncertainty interval: 71.02–83.33), with the highest increases observed in Africa, the Eastern Mediterranean, and South-East Asia. By 2021, global HF prevalence reached 7,048.1 thousand cases. Males consistently showed higher disease burden than females. Ischaemic heart disease was identified as the leading contributor to HF burden. Projections indicate a continued increase to 8,115.4 thousand cases by 2035.

### Conclusion

HF burden among adolescents and young adults has significantly increased globally from 1990 to 2021, with marked regional and sex-specific differences. Targeted prevention strategies are needed based on socioeconomic and regional disparities.
