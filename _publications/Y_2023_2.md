## Abstract

**Background and Objective:**  
Model selection and accounting for uncertainty in model choice are central challenges in statistical inference. This study compares multiple Bayesian model averaging (BMA) approaches to identify the most efficient and accurate methods for financial econometric modeling in Iran’s asset markets.

**Methods:**  
Ten Bayesian averaging approaches were evaluated. In addition, principal component regression (PCR) was applied using simulation studies across different datasets to assess the relationship between liquidity and asset prices. Key financial variables included liquidity, exchange rate, oil income, stock price index, and housing price index.

**Results:**  
Principal component analysis identified four main components, with the first explaining more than 95% of the variance. Liquidity and exchange rate were found to be the most influential variables in stock price index modeling, while liquidity, exchange rate, and oil income significantly affected housing price index models. PCR combined with AIC-based model selection demonstrated the best performance among competing approaches.

**Conclusion:**  
The results indicate that principal component regression combined with Bayesian model averaging improves model performance. The findings suggest a strong positive relationship between liquidity and asset prices, providing useful insights for monetary policy decision-making.
